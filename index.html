What the map is — and is not

This project treats the New Testament as 27 distinct ancient documents, following the historical-critical spirit of Dale B. Martin’s Yale course. Quantitative dimensions are measurements or approximate scholarly ranges. Interpretive dimensions are deliberately transparent editorial estimates on a 0–100 scale.

Date bars: broad scholarly ranges, intentionally wide where dating is contested. Greek length: approximate word counts; counts vary slightly by critical edition. Interpretive scores: comparative placements meant to provoke close reading, not to collapse scholarly disagreement into a fake precision.

Each selected document includes a short rationale and passages worth checking. Future versions can attach competing scholarly models to the same axis rather than pretending there is only one map.
